TechJam Track 4 — Conversational Shopping Agent

An agent that asks the right question before it guesses the right product.

Four days, five people, one 50,000-product catalog, and an evaluator that scores a shopping conversation on whether the target product shows up in the top 10, how high it ranks, and how many turns it took. This page is the honest record: what we built, what we measured, what we tried and threw away, and where the number could be gamed versus where it's real.

The problem, in plain language

What the agent actually has to do

A simulated customer sends a short message, up to 10 turns. On every turn the agent returns up to 10 product IDs and may ask one clarifying question. The conversation stops the moment the customer's (hidden) target product appears in that top 10 — or after turn 10, which counts as a miss. Four kinds of customer: buying (states one hard requirement up front), browsing (vague, "still exploring"), intent override (changes their mind partway through), and boundary (has no preference on whatever you ask about). The scored metric is:

TechnicalScore = 0.50 × HitRate@10 + 0.30 × MRR + 0.20 × Efficiency, where Efficiency = clip((11 − MTTC) / 10, 0, 1) and MTTC is the mean turn of first hit (a miss counts as turn 11). A random-guess starter agent scores 0.107. We shipped 0.891.
BM25

A 1970s-vintage keyword search formula, still the industry default. It scores a document higher when it contains the query's words, weighted by how rare those words are across the whole catalog (a match on "spandex" counts for more than a match on "the").

Query "95% cotton, 5% spandex" → any product whose feature list contains that near-exact phrase jumps to the top, because those tokens are rare and the phrase itself is rarer still.
Embedding / dense retrieval

Instead of matching words, a neural model converts text into a vector of numbers such that similar meanings end up close together in that space — even with zero shared vocabulary.

"warm winter coat" and "insulated parka" share no words but land near each other in embedding space. We use this (bge-small) as a candidate route, disabled in the shipped config — see Results.
MRR (Mean Reciprocal Rank)

1 ÷ (the rank the correct product landed at), averaged across sessions. A hit at rank 1 scores 1.0; a hit at rank 10 scores 0.1; a miss scores 0. It rewards ranking the right answer first, not just getting it in the list somewhere.

Two agents both hit 100% of the time, but one always ranks the target #1 (MRR 1.0) and the other always ranks it #8 (MRR 0.125) — same HitRate, very different MRR.

Statistical discipline

At n=200, an unpaired bootstrap puts the standard error on TechnicalScore around 0.0096 — a 95% band roughly ±0.019 wide. Two configs run on the same 200 sessions are correlated, though, so comparing them as if they were independent overstates the uncertainty. We instead resample per-session outcomes with a shared resample index across both configs (paired bootstrap) — B=10,000 iterations, seed 20260831 — which cancels the shared variance and answers "would config A beat config B on a resample of this population," directly.

Paired bootstrap, 95% CI on the score delta
Dot = mean delta over 10,000 resamples. Bar = 95% CI. Crossing the dashed zero line means "not significant at this sample size" — not "no effect."
The bucket question doesn't have one answer — and that's the honest one. Isolated cleanly against a dense-on baseline (both configs carry dense=0.20, only bucket differs), removing bucket costs +0.0122, CI [0.0065, 0.0182] — significant. Compared directly against the actually-shipped config (dense=0 on both sides — the literal wording of the Day-4 brief), the same removal costs +0.0069, CI [−0.0039, 0.0185] — not significant. Bucket's marginal value appears to interact with whether dense is also active; against the configuration we actually ship, dropping it is not proven to hurt. We're reporting both framings rather than the one that flatters either conclusion.
Worked example of why paired beats unpaired. CLAUDE.md's original unpaired heuristic called "beats BM25-only" a +0.014 delta "within noise" (below the ~0.019 band). The properly paired test says otherwise: CI [0.0078, 0.0483], clearly excluding zero. Same data, same two configs — the unpaired test was simply too conservative, exactly as the methodology predicts.
